How it works

Three steps, all on your machine.

  1. Install and connect

    Homer and a local AI model are installed on your Mac. You sign in to your Google account once. During the beta we set it up with you.

  2. Ask in plain English

    Type what you need in Homer's chat, which opens in your browser and runs on your computer. The model turns your sentence into a clear request; plain code finds the email, the thread or the free slot.

  3. Approve what goes out

    Homer drafts. You read it, edit it by asking, and press Send, or reject it and nothing happens.

Consent

Nothing is sent until you press Send.

  • Sending an email or changing your calendar always becomes a card like this one. The model writes the draft; it has no way to send it.
  • The rule lives in the server code, not in the AI's instructions. Approving checks the card has not changed, and a double click cannot send twice.
  • Ask for changes in chat ("make it warmer") and the same card updates.
  • On our test laptop (M3 Pro, 18 GB) the median time from your message to a ready draft was 4.4 seconds.

Who it is for

Built for professionals with confidential clients.

If you are a lawyer at a small firm, an advisor working under NDA or an accountant, your inbox is full of things your clients trusted you with. Most AI email tools work by sending that mail to an AI company. That is one more party holding client information, and one more thing to explain to the client.

Homer is built so that the AI part stays on your computer, and so that you can see where your data went. The log on the Privacy page is something you can look at, not a promise on a website.

We are looking for a small number of firms on Google Workspace to try it with us, set up together, and tell us what it gets wrong.

Context, not advice. The American Bar Association's Formal Opinion 512 (2024) discusses lawyers' duties when using generative AI, including confidentiality and when client consent is needed before client information goes into an AI tool.

Questions

Honest answers.

Do I need a powerful computer?

Yes, a fairly recent one. Homer needs an Apple Silicon Mac with 16 GB of memory or more (18 GB or more is comfortable) and about 30 GB of free disk. A Linux machine with an 8 GB graphics card also works. The first question after starting takes about 10 seconds longer while the model loads. Slower machines are slower.

Does Google see my mail?

Yes. It is your Gmail, so Google already has it, and Homer reads it through Google's official API. What Homer adds is no new AI company: the model that reads and drafts runs on your computer, and your mail is not sent to OpenAI, Anthropic, Google's AI services or anyone else for processing.

Is the AI as good as ChatGPT?

No. It is a smaller model (Qwen 3.5, 9 billion parameters) that fits on a laptop. It is good at reading, short drafts and understanding requests; it is weaker at long reasoning. That is why plain code does the lookups and every action ends with your approval.

Is it free?

During the beta, yes. There is no per-token bill because the model runs on your machine. We will talk about pricing with beta users before anyone is asked to pay.

Does it work on Windows?

Not yet. Windows is planned. Today Homer runs on macOS and Linux.

Does it work with Outlook?

Not today. Homer works with Gmail and Google Calendar, and works best for teams on Google Workspace. Tell us on the form if you use Outlook; it helps us decide what to build next.

What languages does it support?

English only, for now.
